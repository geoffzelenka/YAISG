## 1. Overview

This documents contains the vision for YAISG (Yet Another Idle Skilling Game). It describes how the game and its mechanics work, while leaving the implementation details to the design.md.  YAISG is a multiplayer, web-based idle skilling game (similar to Melvor Idle, Idle Clans, Ironwood RPG, etc).  Players train skills in real time via timed action cycles to accumulate experience points (XP) and resources.  The main differentiator for YAISG is the existence of a World Map that is divided into Realms.  The player also develops and maintains their own estate.  The estate may be a mobile (a small camp or caravan) or fixed in a chosen realm.


## 2. Overarching Features and Mechanics

1. XP Curve and Tiers
  a. 1 curve shared among all skills, time to max out a skill dependent on access and cycle time
  b. 5 Tiers of skill and goods values, unlocked by achieving a set level in the skill
2. Skills
  a. Skills turn time (and optionaly input goods) into goods.  
  b. Current vision contains 6 classes of skills:
    i. Gathering - strictly time into raw material
   ii. Refining - time + raw material into materials that feed manufacturing
  iii. Manufacturing - time + goods turns into fnished goods
   iv. Combat - Melee, Ranged, Magic, Defense, Accuracy, Vigor for performing Player Vs Environment combat encounters
    v. Exploration - this skill turns time into access keys
   vi. Travelling - Lowers the time it takes to move between realms, gates certain realms and can open shortcuts
3. Goods
  a. Goods are also divided into Tiers and drive the economy
  b. Gathered goods can have regional flavor, refined goods converge back into shared commodities
  c. Refining and Manufacturing require facilties from Realms or Estates to perform
4. Combat
  a. Turns time and optional consumables into combat skill xp and loot drops.
  b. Combat is a contest between the player's and combatants skills that resolve cycle by cycle: hits land or miss wearing down hit points, consumables are consumed, and the contest ends when one contestent's hit points reach 0. Then the combatent is respawned or the player retires from the field to heal.
  b. economically, combat is similar to a gathering skill

  a. One world divided into many realms that contain player estates.
  b. Realms have a type and are distinct from each other.

    i. Mint - generate gold from mintable goods (gold ore, monster dropped coin purses, jewels, etc)
  d. Realms follow a graph and can morph horizontally or upgrade through player launched project and upgrade campaigns, however a realm that cannot maintain its upkeep will degrade and can eventually downgrade
  e. Available gathering and combat spots are content defined.
  f. Available refining and manufacturing facilities are dependent on the player's estate and completed realm projects
  g. Realms may have players initiated Upgrades and Projects 
  h. Player estates follow a similar upgrade graph and also require upkeep
  i. Players estates also feed into the Realms marketplace
  j. Players may sell estates to its containing realm
  k. Players may build new estates or purchase available estates from the realm
6. Estates
  a. Mobile Estates
  b. Fixed Estates
  c. Creating and Upgrading Estates
  d. Estate tiers
  e. Estate sales
  f. Estate market mechanics
7. Market and Economy
  a. Goods are only produced via player skill actions and estates production
  b. Currency is only generated by realms with a mint upgrade that turn mintable goods into gold
  c. There are 2 markets, the global player market and the local realm market
  d. Global player market:
    i. Only players may access the player market - however, the realm the player is in must have the upgrade that attaches it to the market (access roads/waterways and some sort of town type)
   ii. Player gets escrow slots that are used for buying and selling on the player marketplace, provides sane limit to orders and a place to hold the goods and gold for (partial) fills
  e. Realm market:
    i. Serves to churn the world goods market - it buys goods needed to serve realm upkeep and sells goods from its stores that its realm identity produces

## 3. XP Curve, Levels, and Tiers
### 3.0 Summary
Skills go from Level 1 (0 XP) to 100 (10,000,000 XP) spanning 5 tiers along an exponential curve 2^(1/7).

One curve, shared across all skills, that turns time spent skilling into levels and levels into tiers.  The curve doubles in cost every 7 levels, meaning level 93 is the halfway point to level 100.
### 3.1 Levels
- Characters start every skill at level 1 with 0 XP accumulated
- The Maximum level at release will be 100.  XP will still accumulate, however, after level 100 there is no new level or other game mechanic relevant reward
### 3.2 Tiers and thresholds
- At launch there will be 5 tiers that represent skill aptitude with thresholds of:
    Tier 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 / 5 unlock at levels 1 / 50 / 75 / 90 / 95
- Tier titles are content defined

### 3.3 The XP Curve
#### 3.3.1 The function
XP required to advance from L to L+1:
**xp(L) = floor(A · r^L)**, for L = 1 … 99, with **r = 2^(1/7) ≈ 1.10409** and **A chosen so that total XP to level 100 = 10,000,000** (A ≈ 52)

Reference points along the curve: Level 1-2 = 58XP; 50-51 = 7,200XP, 90-91 = 370k XP, 99-100 = 940k XP.

#### 3.3.2 Time Budget
Three anchors to feed how the skill curve should *feel*:
- First, using only the level 1 skill action - 2,880 hours (4 months) to level 100
- Optimal skill advancment and no resource constraints - ~720 hours
- Fully-bonused and optimal play - ~500 hours - which fixes a cap of +45% bonus to a skill

#### 3.3.3 Skilling XP bonus cap
Total from all sources - estate upgrade, realm modifiers, realm-tier bonueses, equipment bonuses, consumables should cap out at +45%.

### 3.4 Tier Proficiency
#### 3.4.1 What and why
A veteran in a skill should be able to finish the skill cycle faster than the neophyte, however with that skill cycle speed bonus there may be a taper in XP earned per cycle

Proficiency skill speed-up steps at tier distances, per family (`cycle_time_pct` reduction):

| Family | 1 below | 2 below | 3 below | 4 below |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gathering | +5% | +10% | +15% | +20% |
| Refining | — | +5% | +10% | +20% |
| Manufacturing | — | +10% | +20% | +30% |
| Combat | combat-effectiveness bonuses vs. under-tier combatants (excluding Vigor), not cycle-time — Combat 0.3 §3.5; cycle time stays pure weapon speed | | | |
| Travel / Exploration | reaching-tier grants, not below-tier proficiency (§10) | | | |

Diminishing returns kick in as follows:
- Gathering: no diminishing returns at any distance.
- Refining: xp tapers from 2 tiers below the character's tier; 3 tiers below and beyond, xp is zero.
- Manufacturing: xp tapers at 1 tier below; 2 tiers below and beyond, xp is zero.
- Exploration and Travel: no diminishing returns.
- Combat: the offensive xp stream tapers by tier distance against under-tier combatants (distances/steps content/balance); the defensive stream attenuates naturally (weak combatants deal little damage) and needs no taper; drop tables never diminish. Note combat's taper applies to stream earnings inside the resolver, not as generic `xp_pct` 

Tapering of Refining and Manufacturing is to encourage veteran players to continue to produce goods at the top of their skill level and to work on improving the realms to accept those goods to create markets.  Since Gathering has no resource inputs, gathering low-level goods would be to feed an economic need at the expense of XP efficiency.

### 3.5 Unlock cadence and content density
- Activity definitions are the content designer's responsibility
- Gathering, Refining, and Manufacturing aim for a new activity every 10 levels from tiers 1-3
- Gathering, Refining, and Manufacturing at tiers 4 and 5 should have new activities unlock quicker and offer more horizontal choices

### 3.6 Skill family baselines and access
- the section 3.3.1 defines skill baselines (2,880 hours, 720 hours, 500 hours), however the "feel" of how each skill works can be tuned with an xp/hour and skill cycle tuning via content definition or assumptions of player skill cycle bonuses.
- Access is a large block to being able to achieve truly optimal skilling, realms that contain high tier gathering spots, facilities from realms or estates to refine and manufacture

### 3.7 Combat leveling
- Tier multiplier - for Melee, Ranged, Magic, Accuracy, and Defense a uniform +5% per tier bonus for each skill.  Vigor remains static (it is not involved in any combatent contests)
- Combat xp is a two stream, two pool model.
  1. Offensive stream: XP gain based on damage dealt (up to the offensive xp pool maximum) and goes to Attack style (Melee, Ranged, Magic) and accuracy skills and tapers by tier distance between opponents
  2. Defensive stream: XP gain based on damage taken/mitigated (up to the defensive xp pool maximum) and goes to Vigor and Defense skills, tapers naturally as less damage is taken as the player progresses

### 3.8 Travel and Exploration
- Travel tier-ups grant faster journey-time and reduction to the per-edge supply costs.  
- Travel tier level may gate access to a certain map-egde, thus more experienced travellers know where short-cuts are, or can brave a more treachorous route.
- Exploration tier-ups grant faster expedition cycles and more keys per discovery and the higher tier skews the exploration drop table to have more rare keys available


## 4 Skills
### 4.1 Skills summary
Skills turn time into goods and goods and skills climb a ladder of tiers that the whole economy is based on.  Each skill family has a definied economic role, what it consumes and what it supplies.  Nothing produces into the void and nothing consumes from nowhere.  A skill that doesn't participate in the economy loop is a design mistake.

The production spine is Gathering -> Refining -> Manufacturing.  Combat is a risk-flavored producer-consumer and exploration is a supplier of access through keys.

Refining and Manufacturing also require facilities to perform those skills - these facilities are made available through the Realm the player is currently in or fron the player's Estate

### 4.2 Skill Categories
- Gathering - turns time into basic goods, Mining yields ore; Lumberjacking yields logs; farming produces grains, vegetables, etc.  Gathering is where realm flavor may live
- Refining - Turns gathered goods plus time into intermediate goods.  Smelting turns ore to ingots, milling turns logs into planks. Refinig converges local flavored goods into commodities
- Manufacturing - turns refined goods, monster drops and produces finished goods.  Alchemy takes herbs and monster parts to make potions, Weaponsmithing takes ingots and planks to make swords, etc.  Manufacturing also produces generic tools and generic goods that are required for estate and realm upkeep.
- Combat- producer-consumer hybrid - consumes consumables like potions and food and produces items from a combatents drop table (eg leather, coin purses, spider fangs, etc)
- Exploration - Turns time spent into access: discovers reource nodes, monster dens, etc in the form of keys that can be sold to other players
- Travel - Time spent traveling that opens up access to frontier realms, and faster and cheaper travel

### 4.2.2 Category Rules
- Every skill requires an economic role, skills outputs must be an input to something
- Production chains cross realms by design.  A realm will rarely have enough inputs to do a complete chain, especially of higher tier goods
- Categories are content groupings and not enging types - engine sees actions with cycle times, inputs, outputs, and availability gates.  Categories exist for content design and UI gropuings

### 4.2.3 Proficiency: mastery of fundamentals
Players with higher skill levels can work faster - a level 80 blacksmith can make a level 10 item faster than a level 10 blacksmith.

- Threshold based, stepping at tier boundaries.  Bonus applies based on tier distance step-function, not a continuous curve.  Different skill categories have different proficiency rules. 
- Speed is the only tier based bonus, no additional yield
- Tier distance also determines XP taper if it is applicable to that skill category

## 5 Goods
### 5.1 Goods Summary
- Goods are items generated from skills.
- All goods can be generated via some skill's action
- Goods are all designed by the content creator
- All goods are assigned a Tier, that tier should come from the skill that generated it (eg Tier 3 skill should create a Tier 3 good, but may use inputs that are Tier 1, 2, or 3.  Generally higher tiered skill cycles are longer than lower tiered cycles.
- Goods will have tags that help categorize their use, a pine log and a palm log may both be tier 1 and tagged as logs, so that a tier 1 lumber milling skill can turn either of those items into a tier 1 wooden plank, or a lumber mill themed estate that requires tier 1 logs for upkeep would accept either the pine or palm log.
- Gathered goods may have realm specific flavor, but once they have been refined the refined good has become a commodity.


## 6 Combat
### 6.1 Combat Summary
Combat turns time and consumables into combat skill xp and loot drops.  Each encounter is a contest between the player's stats, equipment and bonuses against a combatant's stats, resolved cycle by cycle: hits land or miss; damage wears down hit point, consumables are used, and when one side's hit points are exhausted the encounter ends -- in loot and a respawn, or in the player retiring from the field to heal.

Combat is econmically a gathering skill with risk attached: combatents have drop tables for goods used for other skills.

### 6.2 Combat Loop
#### 6.2.1 Engagement
The player engages in combat by choosing a combat location within the Realm ( an inn's basement, a hunting ground, deep cave, or a bandit hideout) and a combatent to fight.  The encounter then occurs using the existing cycle framework, each cycle being one round of combat

#### 6.2.1 The Combat Cycle
Combat cycle length derives from weapon speed plus applicable modifies and resolves as such:
1. Hit resolution - who hit whom, and for how much damage
2. XP award - each combatent has 2 pools of XP (offense and defense) that award in 2 streams, offensive xp is awarded based on damage dealt and the defensive xp awarded based on damage taken and mitigated.
3. Consumable resolution:
   - Utility slot item (ammunition, weapon oil, magic components) are consumed per cycle
     - A ranged attacker that depletes their supply of ammunition shall retreat from the field
     - Melee and Magic may keep fighting, but be less effective without their utility slot consumables
   - Reactive consumables (potions, food) are consumed in repsonse to player state, eg low health or a buff expiring
4. Terminal checks --
 - Combatant HP exhausted: award loot from the combatants drop table; wait for a respawn timer, and then begin combat cycles again
 - The player's HP is exhausted: the player retires to being idle.  The action ends.  Death does not cost anything more besides the player's state becoming idle.


#### 6.2.2  Offline resolution
Combat will resolve itself lazily like every other action, but it has to account for the combat state machine instead of uniform cycles, hit points and consumable stacks, kill/respawn alternations, the player going losing and going idle. The player should receive a summary of report when returning from being offline.


###  6.3 Idle health regeneration
Players that are missing health will slowly regenerate health

## 7 Realms 
### 7.1 Realms summary
Realms are not parallel servers, but different regions on a single shared map.  The map helps players realize they're part of an interconnected world and allow them to sense they have a place.  The maps makes travel feel real and not an arbitrary time-gate, and allow the players a way to view the evolution of the world.  Each realm is its own presence-gated market within the server's economy, goods store and treasure differentials that move goods amongst realms via npc haulers, and have their very own realm flavor.  

### 7.2 Realms types and tiers
Each realm has its own economy identity based on its content-defined geography and how far along its tier web it has evolved.  The realm types will give availability and bonuses to some skills and possibly a malus to other skills and certain skills may not even be availabe (eg fishing in a desert realm)  Some example realm types would be:

- Agrarian realm - farming and cooking and other food type skills and resources
- Mountain realm - mining, smelting, metal crafting bonuses
- Forest realm - Lumberjacking, plank milling, and carpentry - end-to-end timber economy, hunting flabored combat encounters
- Desert realm - mining and glass industry - 
- Industrial realn - trees have been felled, fields paved over- but trade and industry flourish here
- Barren / Wilderness realms - frontier realm or a realm that has regressed and decayed - few economic bonuses, but dangerous fauna to hunt/combat, resources to prospect, and room to build or recover what once was

Realms can become more specialized as they travel up the tiers as players perform realm projects.  Realms can transition horizontally more easily at low tiers, and not at all once highly specialized.

No realm can do everything itself, but tier 1 realms may be self-sustainable, but once a realm heads down the road of specialization it will depend more and more on other realm types to feed its goods needs.

### 7.3 Upkeep
Developed realms have an upkeep, goods that are needed to maintain the realm. As realms complete projects and have estates built they will develop upkeep costs.  This is the goods sink that keeps goods circulating and provides asink to all the stuff created by players and their estates.

As a realm further specializes, it will requires more goods from other industries to maintain that specialization.

### 7.4 Realm evolution
The realm identity is earned by the players that populate that realm.  Realms have a set of projects that work like public quests to steer the identity and bonuses/maluses of the realm.  As enough projects complete a vote may be kicked off among realm residents to morph the realm along an edge of its development graph.  An example of this owould be a forested realm's populace constructing sawmills and moving towards an industrialized edge vs clear cutting forests and replanting fields to move towards an agrarian realm.

### 7.5 Decay and regression
If realms miss quotas and its stores run dry the realm will start to decay.  First, expensive realm upgrades will decay and no longer function, and then ultimately regress to a lower tier until the realm can meet equillibrium again, or totally collapse into a wasteland or barren realm.

Decay is a way to self-correct realms advancing too quickly or becoming stale if the population moves on to other areas of the map.  It allows for a frontier area to re-establish.

Decay is slow process that should be easily seen by players.

### 7.6 Realm tiers and the goods pyramid
As a realm tiers up, the goods required form a pyramid.  New tiers of goods are added to the top, and the previous tiers of goods are expanded upon.  The base needs to get larger to support the taller pyramid.  This way tier one goods stay relevent forever.  And, as the realm becomes more specialized, it will require a broader set of lower tier goods as well.

### 7.6 Realm projects and upgrades
Projects are a discrete community activity that a player my start and then any player inside the realm may contribute to.  The realm may also contribute to projects if they have excess goods in the realm's stores.  Example projects could be:
- Construct a road - first step to connecting a wilderness realm to the rest of the world 
- Establish trade routes - allow players in the realm to be active in the global player market
- Build a lumber mill - create the facility to allow lumber milling into planks
- Construct a city walls - a step to tier up a realm 
- Dig the mine deeper - a step to upgrade realm along a mining tier

Upon completion of a project the realm gains a permenant tag that grants access, bonuses, and/or maluses

Realm Upgrades are how a realm morphs into another, either tiering up or a horizontal move to a different type.  The realm morph graph will define the requirements that must be met before such an uprade can happen.  Since upgrade are a major change, a realm-wide vote is kicked off before the upgrade can occur.

#### 7.6.1 Project lifecycles
The content designer creates various projects for each realm type in the realm development graph.  This means each realm type can only offer projects designed for it to move along edges of its graph node.  A player in the realm can kickoff a project by paying its kickoff cost in gold and goods.  Projects need to be flagged if only residents may kickoff or if residents and visitors may kickoff.

Once a project has been kicked off, its total cost must be met.  Players in the realm may contribute as much as they like to the project.  Projects will also add a goods draw to the realm, so that a realm may be able to passively complete projects through its own market draw mechanics.

A project that has not been contributed to for a while will start to decay from its contributions and ultimately fail using the same mechanic as realm decay..  A failed project will enter a significan cooldown (a day or two) period before it can be begun again.  Goods used by projects are consumed, just like realm upkeep, thus no goods are refunded.

Once a project has completed, it will move to a build phase which is an amount of time before the tag is added to the realm.

A player may only kickoff 1 project a day in the world.

A realm may only have one active project at a time.

There should be a log about who has kicked off a project and contributors, that will be attached to the tag when added to the realm, so that players can have some meta game bragging rights about project contrbutions

#### 7.6.2 Realm Upgrade Lifecycle
Once enough projects have been completed to perform a Realm Upgrade a resident of the realm may request that the upgrade begin.  The player must post a significant fee to begin the Realm Upgrade process. Since Realm Upgrades cam have major ramifications to a realm, the residents (players that own an estate) of a realm may vote on if the realm upgrade will happen.  The player's type of estate lends weight to their vote as follows:

| Estate | Weight |
| --- | --- |
| mobile type (tent, wagon, boat ,etc) | 1 |
| fixed form tier 1 | 10 |
| fixed form above tier 1 | +1 (10% of base) - tier 2 = 11, tier 3 = 12, ... |

A player may only vote once and the ballot is secret, but participation in the vote is public. A player is only eligible to vote if they were a resident of the realm when the vote was called.  The default vote position is to abstain, there is no minimum quorum needed, thus 1 vote Against and the rest of the residents abstaining means the vote fails.  A vote lasts a few days, if all possible residents cast a vote, the vote may end early.

If a vote fails, there is cooldown before another realm upgrade vote may be called.  The fee is still forfeit to the realm's treasury regardless of the vote outcome.

If the vote succeeds, the realm starts a realm upgrade procedure.  For a week the realm is in a transitory state, no projects or upgrade votes may be called.  Keys to that realm will be marked deprecated, but will only fail once the upgrade is completed.

#### 7.6.2 Realm decaying
If a realm decays it should unwind that stack of project upgrades and then tier upgrades.  When am uprade decays its tag is removed from the realm.  If the realm degrades enough to lower its tier, it will enter a transitory period similar to the realm upgrade process.

## 8 Estates
Estates are how a player can specialize themselves.  The player must choose between a mobile estate or a fixed one, and then how they will specialize as they move up the Estate upgrade graph

### 8.1 Mobile Estates
Mobile estate represent things like a humble tent, a traders caravan, or a boat.  They let the bring their estate bonuses with them by relocating with the player.  These estates would be for the traveller, adventurer, trader, or developer playstyles.

### 8.2 Fixed Estates
Fixed estates become a set piece of a realm's economy.  They allow the player to gain significant bonuses and create goods for the realms economy.  Fixed estates represent a things like a farmstead, a forge-hall, a lumber mill, workshop, or a even their own keep.  The trade-off for choosing a fixed-estate is more franchisement in realm votes vs mobility.

### 8.3 Estates and Realms
Estates interact with Realms, both Estates and Realms have a larder and upkeep needs.  Estates also generate goods that feed directly into the Realm larders.  Estate bonus work best when paired with a similar Realm type, such that the Forge-hall in the ore rich mountainous realm.  Should estate bonuses stack multiplicitly with realm bonuses?  Similarlry a fishmongers wharf should not work very well in a desert realm bereft of any water.  Tier 1 realms should be able to have their needs met by a few tier 1 estates.

### 8.4 Creating an Estate and Upgrade graph
The specific kinds of Estates and their upgrade graph is under the purview of the content designer.  Realms could have local flavor for available estates.  Estate syle blueprints could be an interesting drop type.  Players can then build a valid tier 1 estate in a realm, at hefty cost that would mean the player has graduated from the early game to the mid-game.  The tier 1 fixed-estate should be a generalist within what a realm offers, as it upgrades it should begin to specialize.  Estate build and upgrade require goods of the same tier it belongs to as well as some lower tiered goods as well.

### 8.5 Estate tiers
Estates get better the more they are invested in.  Mobile-estates travel better and reduce needed travel supplies and produce some of its own travel supplies or goods along the way.  Fixed estates get better at producing their specific manufactured or refined goods and give better bonuses.  

### 8.6 Estate Production and Upkeep
Estates also have an upkeep, they need input materials as well as general goods to maintain the estate and its followers, workers, wagons, etc.  The player may load the larders of the Estate, and the Estate will also attempt to draw from the realm when the larder runs out.  The same decay mechanism that exists for Realms apply to Estates.

### 8.7 Estate sales
Realms must be bootstrapped with NPC estates to get production going.  Players may buy NPC estates from the realm, the cost of these estates should be relative to the cost of the input goods required to build the estate. Players that no longer want to own their estate may surrender it to the Realm, the realm will attempt to pay as much it can for Estate, however the player may choose to donate the estate to the realm for no charge.  The value of the estate will be calculated based on the cost to rebuild it at the market price the realm sees for the goods (or at the content defined cost of the good, if the realm cannot see that good).  The realm will sell estates at the estate value with a defined percentage on top  so that players cannot make money buying and reselling to the realm.  However, a developer archtype player could work to build up estates and sell them to a realm and buyback to jump start a wilderness realm's development.

If a player abandons the game and thus their estate, the estate will function as an NPC estate that cannot be sold.  The estate will still be subject to decay rules, and if it falls apart, then it is gone.

### 8.8 Estate market mechanics
#### 8.8.1 Estates tick with the realm
Estate passive production is processed as the realm is processed, the player needs to take no action.

#### 8.8.2 Estates transact in goods
Estates hold no wallet, just goods.  It credits its goods production to the realm's store and takes its upkeep goods from its own larder and then from the realm's.

#### 8.8.3 Estate gauges
Esates will have a deficit gauge that functions similarly to the realms' deficity gauge detailed later on.

#### 8.8.4 Estate larder
A prepaid upkeep buffer, not goods storage. This is to enable a player to prepay if they know they will be absent or just to not worry about the estate decaying.  The player can only deposit, no goods can come out of this.

#### 8.8.9 Estate decay
Estate decay will function the same as Realm decay, detailed below.

## 9 Market and Economy
### 9.1 Summary
There are two markets, the global player market and the realm-based markets.  The global player market acts as world-wide order book for players to trade gold for goods instantly over the order book.  The global player market seeks to maximizze liquidity and price-discovery.  The realm-markets are the economic engine to drive supply and demand for all the generated goods.  Realm markets buy what the realm demands and sells what the realm has excess of, from the realm's own stores with prices that rise with scarcity, but only with gold its own economy has earned.

Players trade on the global markey to move goods between players, while realm markets trade to sustain the world itself.

### 9.2 The Global Player Market
#### 9.2.1 One book, whole world
There is a single global order book per good.  Goods and gold match instantly, no transport time.  The goal is to maximize liquidity and price-discovery that scales with the world population.  It is expected that players can arbitrage between the global market and the realm markets.

#### 9.2.2 Full order book, buy and sell orders
- Buy orders, player names a good, a quantity, and a unit price.  The gold required to fill the buy order is placed in that orders escrow slot when the order is creted.
- Sell orders, player names a good, a quantity, and unit price.  The goods are moved into the player's escrow slot when the order is creted.
- Cancellation - returns remaining escrow and any partial-fills to the players inventory
- Fulfillment - pays the seller the order's value less the realm tax of the realm the order was created in to the realm the order was created in. The buyer receives the goods into the player's order slot.

Players may see the entire order book.

#### 9.2.3 Order slots
Players have a set amount of order slots.  These slots serve to hold escrow gold or goods when creating an order, and serve as a basket to hold (partial) fills.  Players then collect order fills from those order slots.

So the order lifecycle is 
1) Create an order in an empty slot
2) escrow flows out as order is filled
3) order slot enters collection mode when order is completed or cancelled
4) player collects goods and/or gold to empty the slot
5) Slot ready for a new order

Order slots are a capped resource to serve primarily as an anti-manipulation tactic, and also to allow a valuable resource that could be made available through late game estate upgrades or as a premium upgrade.

#### 9.2.4 Global Market Seller Taxes
Every order match pays a flat, content defined global tax rate out of the sellers proceeds that is credited to the treasury of the realm that the player was in when the sell order was created.

Flat, global rate to prevent all realms racing to the bottom of tax rates.  It only applies to the seller-side since that is where the money is ending up.  The tax goes to the realm where the seller's presence was, since that realm facilitated the trade.  And this tax is one of the few ways for realms to fill their coffers.

#### 9.2.5 Global market access is earned, and losable

Realms only have access to the global market by having the "market-access" tag.  That tag is one of the early-tier realm development upgrades.  And as such, can be lost if that realm sufficiently decays.

This serves a gameplay reason as it creates an economic cost to venturing out into the wilderness.

#### 9.2.6 
The global markets full order book is visible to all player anywhere.  The market-access tag only prevents players creating orders on the global market.

### 9.3 Realm Markets
### 9.3.1 Market Function: sink and engine

Each realm operates a marketplace that
1) Buys the goods required fill its stores in order to feed its upkeep demand and active project demand.  
2) Sells goods that are created from its realm identity's upgrades and hosted estates production from the realm market's stores.  

Realm markets cannot mint items from thin air, goods must be produced by players, realm upgrades, estate upgrades.

### 9.3.2 Prices

Realm-market pricing has three layers:
1. Reference prices - content defined price of the good as set by the content designer.
2. The bid-curve - What a realm offers for a good is a function of the reference price and the good's larder level.  When the larder is full, the price is near the floor and climbs as the larder empties out.  Distance also adds to this cost, the player in the realm incurs to no distance penalty.  The wealthy, flush realm across the map incurs a higher cost penalty than the neigboring realm with a modest surplus.
3. Treasury - the bid-curve is what a realm is willing to pay, however, a realm cannot mint gold out of thin air to pay.  The available treasury of the realm caps what it can pay for a good.  A realm cannot bid an amount that it cannot pay.

### 9.3.3 Cold start
A fresh world needs goods and gold in circulation, otherwise the realm deadlocks itself and fades away before players can begin.  The content-designer must seed a world that has some semblence of balance and sustainability.

### 9.3.5 Presence
Trading with a realm's market requires the player to be in that realm.  Either by living or visiting the realm.

### 9.4 Inter-realm realm market mechanics
#### 9.4.1 Summary
Periodically realm markets balance unmet needs amongst each other, treasury-to-treasury, with goods paying a transport cost that scales with distance.  

The realm-to-realm NPC trade is a seconday choice.  Realms can only ship a limited amount of goods at a time between each other using NPC haulers.  These NPC haulers take time for goods to travel, and may be further constrained by realm maluses on transit.

#### 9.4.2 Primitives for this mechanic
1) Draw `draw[r,g]` = realm `r`'s consumption of good `g` per day.  Derived from the estates and realm tags and applied per tick as a fraction.  The draw vector is the realm's expressed goods appetite, active realm projects will add a small temporary increaase to realm draw.

2) Reserve `reserve[r,g] = R x draw[r,g]` Where `R` is the amount of runway days.  The reserve line creates a point where demanded goods will not be for sale at any price, nor available to private estates, nor available for projects.  The line defines when a realm has a good, and when it can spare a good.

3) Cap `cap[r,g] = C x draw[r,g]` - the limit to how much of a good a realm may have where `C` is a number of days

4) Hail Cost `haul_cost(a,b)` a scalar cost for moving a unit of good between two realms along an edge.

5) Price Level `M[r] = 1 + P * remoteness(r)` a per-realm modifier applied to both sides of a realm's quotes. `remoteness(r) ∈ [0,1]`

#### 9.4.3 Only sell excess

Realms will quote an asking price on a good `g` only if there is an excess and will never trade below its reserve.  A realm will not starve itself to serve a buyer, and "buying a realm dry" won't be a thing

The reserve `R` number of days has a configured default but can be modified with realm modifiers

A pending realm transition will raise the reserve by the number of days required for the transition

#### 9.4.4 Only buy what you need and can afford

If a realm has no draw for a good, it will not bid for that good.

A realm with no treasury, makes no bids.  Similarly, a realm should choose to only bid on goods below reserve in order of the largest draw and only as long as it has funds.


#### 9.4.5 Goods a realm does not consume
For goods where draw = 0, the reserve is 0 so everything may be sold.  However, there must be a hard cap, the warehouse has a roof.

Any production above the hard cap is lost.

#### 9.4.6 Project contributions
The realm is a slow contributor of last-resort.  Active projects will add a demand overlay to any good that project requires.

`overlay_draw[g] = remaining_requirement[g] / T_unaided`

`T_unaided` is a content horizon in days - if the realm was to perform the upgrade all on its own and is content defined.

The realm will only contribute to the project when goods are above its Reserve line.

#### 9.4.7 Inter-realm commerce
If a realm is below its reserve, it should bid for goods at neighboring realms.  The lower the stores are for a particular good, the higher the realm will bid up to the limits of its treasury.  Likewise, a realm with goods in excess of its reserve will set an ask price that lowers at the stores approach the goods Cap.

The inter-realm commerce must take into account realm distance and must prefer nearer neighbords.  To do this a edges travel costs will increase the cost in addition to the ask price of the good.

If a realm completes a purchase, the goods will need to "travel" to the purchasing realm, currency will transfer immediately.  The travel time can be thought of as an NPC caravan travelling. As a risk of travelling across less developed realms, there may be some goods lost due to banditry or bad luck on the road - which could be alleviated with proper realm upgrades.

#### 9.4.8 The Quote function and quantity aware pricing
Let `f = store[r,g] / cap[r,g] ∈ [0,1]`.

```
bid_mult(f) = bid_min + (bid_max − bid_min) × (1 − f)^γ
bid(r,g)    = reference_price[g] × M[r] × bid_mult(f)
ask(r,g)    = bid(r,g) × (1 + m)
```

`γ > 1` gives the desperation shape: flat and cheap across most of the pantry range, spiking only as it empties. Illustrative `bid_min = 0.3`, `bid_max = 2.2`, `γ = 2`, `m = 0.25` — a full realm pays 0.3× reference and sells at 0.375×; an empty one bids 2.2× reference, treasury permitting.

The ask is not an independent curve. Defining it as a markup on the realm's own bid collapses two curves into one, makes the ask inherit the scarcity signal for free (a realm whose stores are draining stops being a cheap source at exactly the moment it starts getting hungry), and — see §5.3 — makes the no-arbitrage constraint a structural property rather than a thing to verify.

`m` is the realm's merchant margin, a global content default with a per-node override. A trade-hub identity running a tighter spread is a good content lever and costs nothing to support.

Realm trades are priced by integrating the curve over the traded quantity, not at spot. Selling 500 grain into a starving realm does not fill 500 units at the empty-store price; it walks the curve up as the store fills, exactly as a large order sweeps an order book. Same on the ask side, walking down.

```
total = reference_price[g] × M[r] × cap[r,g] × ∫[f₀→f₁] bid_mult(f) df
```

For polynomial `bid_mult` this has a closed form — nobody should write a per-unit loop. The treasury clamp (largest `q` whose total is affordable) is a bounded integer binary search.

#### 9.4.8 Upkeep mechanics

The cadence of upkeep ticks are configurable, but should start as hourly.  The content designer should design upkeep costs at the dayly level and then the engine will divide that appropriately to handle upkeep draw.

If travel time of inter-realm NPC commerce grows longer than the tick rate, then realm treasuries will need to know they have pending inventory coming.

Tick ordering:
1. Estate production credits stores.
2. Estate upkeep draws from the estate's own **larder** first (§7.5).
3. Estate upkeep shortfalls draw from realm stores — **but only down to the realm's reserve line.**
4. Realm upkeep draws from stores, and *may* go below the reserve line: the reserve is a floor on what a realm will *spare*, never on what it will *eat*.
5. Project commitment, only from stores above the reserve line.
6. Spoilage.
7. Caps clamp
8. Gauges update — realm and per-estate
9. Decay state machine advances or recovers
10. Regression edge progress accrues or drains

#### 9.4.9 Spoilage
Certain goods will have a content defined spoilage rate, which defaults to 0.  This spoilage will only apply to goods in realm and estate storage.

#### 9.4.10 Solvency Gauge
Not an instantaneous ratio. Per tick:

```
unmet_value    = Σ_g reference_price[g] × (tick_draw[g] − consumed[g])
total_value    = Σ_g reference_price[g] × tick_draw[g]
deficit_ratio  = unmet_value / total_value        (:= 0 when total_value = 0)

gauge += deficit_ratio                            if deficit_ratio > 0
gauge  = max(0, gauge − recovery_rate)            otherwise
```

The gauge's unit is tick-equivalents of total starvation, which is a phrase a player can be shown: "this realm has been short the equivalent of three days."

An accumulator rather than a threshold on a current value gets several things for free that the alternatives make you build. Grace periods are implicit — the counter must build before anything happens. The "which good is missing" problem answers itself, because value-weighting means missing an expensive input accrues faster than missing a cheap one, without a hand-authored weight vector. And min-over-goods (one missing good kills the realm) versus mean (a realm coasts while starving one input) stops being a dilemma, because *duration* carries the weight severity alone cannot.

Per-good coverage (`store / reserve`) stays exposed in the signal layer separately, because *which* good is short is precisely what a hauler needs to know.

#### 9.4.11 The realm decay state machine
| State | Trigger | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Fed** | gauge = 0 | Normal operation. |
| **Strained** | gauge ≥ T1 | **Signal only — no mechanical effect.** Publicly visible on the map and realm panel. |
| **Faltering** | gauge ≥ T2 | Node modifiers scale down by a decay penalty; communal facility tags suspend. Fully reversible, nothing lost. |
| **Failing** | gauge ≥ T3 | Something is shed, at a rate proportional to the gauge. Upgrade-tier rollbacks, with node regression as the last resort. |
| **Regressed** | shedding exhausted | Node swaps backward through the existing transition machinery. |

**Strained must stay purely cosmetic.** It is the recruitment stage — the window in which the signal layer summons haulers and the community notices, before anything is taken away. A realm that suffers the moment it dips is a realm whose players learn to hoard rather than trade.

Recovery walks back down: below `T3`, accrued shedding progress *drains* at a content rate rather than being lost or refunded, so a rescued realm re-climbs from where it fell to. Only completion is irreversible — and even then, the forward path is the ordinary morphing route through projects, which is the renewal cycle working.

### 9.5 Currency
Where does currency gold come from?
- Players have some small seed money
- Realms are bootstrapped with some treasury as part of the initial content definition
- Mint realm upgrades

#### 9.5.1 The mint
A realm upgrade that provides a realm draw for precious metals or other types a goods declare as a "bullion" type.  The realm will then use its existing inter-realm trading mechanism to bid for bullion, 

At each tick the mint will consume buillion and credit its treasury with new coin:

```
coined[r]   = Σ_g  consumed[r,g] × reference_price[g] × K[g]
```

where `K[g]` is the **coinage multiple** — the one new content knob, and the only place in the game where gold comes into existence.

Net gold created per unit of bullion is:

```
seigniorage = reference_price × K  −  bid_paid
```

and `bid_paid` climbs along the bid curve as bullion stores drain. So:

- **Bullion abundant** → stores near cap → bid near `bid_min` → seigniorage wide → the mint coins freely.
- **Bullion scarce** → stores near empty → bid near `bid_max` → seigniorage narrows to zero and then goes negative → **the mint stops being a faucet and becomes a sink.**

With `bid_min = 0.3` and `bid_max = 2.2`, a `K` of around 1.2 makes the mint strongly net-positive on a full pantry and net-negative on an empty one. **The money supply therefore tracks the world's actual mining output automatically**, with no rate limiter to tune and no designer in the loop — which is the §8.6 criterion satisfied by the mechanism's own shape rather than by a cap bolted onto it.

Two further guards, both cheap:

- **`K[g]` is authored as a multiple of `reference_price[g]`, never as an absolute.** A standalone coinage price would be a second, silently disagreeing price scale for the same good, and the two would drift apart the first time a designer repriced one and not the other.
- **Bullion is not exempt from anything.** It spoils at zero like a metal should, it counts toward the deficit gauge like every other unmet draw, and a mint realm that cannot feed its mint accrues gauge and decays — a realm can absolutely starve its own treasury by over-committing to coinage, which is the correct and thematically exact punishment.

**A mint's tier scales its draw**, not its `K`. A bigger mint eats more bullion; it does not conjure more gold per ingot. This keeps `K` a global economic constant rather than a per-upgrade balance surface, and it means mint capacity is bounded by what the world can dig up.

#### 9.5.2 Combat does not drop coin
Combat must not have currency in its drop table, but it may drop bullion item (bandit coin purse, dragon's gold bar hoard, guardian's silver plate, etc).

#### 9.5.3 Currency sinks

- If the coin supply runs too hot, then setting `K < 1` runs the mint backwards.
- Player churn effectively neutralizes a chunk of currency
- Transactions could earn `tax_burn_pct` knob, that will shop at 0.

Otherwise, currency just flows through the realms and players.

#### 9.5.4 Bootsrapping the player
The player will need some way to become economically useful, if the player can come out of tutorial island able to earn coin once they leave the island then maybe they don't need any currency.  Otherwise do all players begin the game chopping down trees to sell to the realm to generate initial currency?



### 9.6 Players and the Realm Economy
#### 9.6.1 Haulers
The player hauler still has a niche to fill either transporting goods from a realm with full stores and sell to desperate ones.  The player hauler can also perform this arbitrage between the Player Market and a needy realm.  Arbitrage can still be severly limited by realm treasury levels.

Frontier realms without global player markets will also require supply runs by players to bootstrap their development.

#### 9.6.2 Other archetypes
- The producer - generates lots of specific type of good and heavily invests their skills and estate into that item.  They then sell on the global book.
- The trader - works the global book
- The adeventurer - buys consumables, heads into the wilderness to secure rare goods and bring back
- The developer - Purpusefully works to develop realms and build estates


## 10 Exploration and Keys
### 10.1 
Exploration turns time and long activity cycles into access: keys to resources that otherwise woulnd't be available.  A key conjures a new lode, a hidden grove, a monster-infested cave that is embodied in the key itself, an instanced artifact that carries the discoverer's name forever and a finite number of accesses.  This item may be sold on a dediated market before its first use, afterwards it is bound to the user until the user chooses to destroy it.

Exploration is a "prestige" skill, it has far longer cycles and discoveries are rare.  Leveling is deliberately slow.

### 10.2 The Exploration Skill
#### 10.2.1 The action loop
Exploration uses the existing cycle framework: repeated search cycles with a discovery drop table with 2 distinguishing properties that are content knobs:
- Cycles are much longer, exploration is measured in expeditions, not swings of an pickaxe.
- Discoveries are rare - most cycles should only yield xp, a discovery itself is a low chance - maybe one or two keys after a night-long skilling session.
- Optional consumables (similar to optional consumables in the melee and magic combat skills) that can give a small targetted boost to shape the drop table (supplies, lures, charts, etc)

#### 10.2.2 Availability and Gating
- Exploration can happen in any realm where the content has defined Exploration is possible, no residence is required, just presence.
- Exploration has cross-skill gates, gathering and combat skill minimums (you must be able to survive the expedition and know and recognize what you find).
- Cross-skill tiers - higher tiers of a players cross skill dependencies can shape the drops table

#### 10.2.3 Discoverable Resources
Discoveries are still content-defined with some random parameters.  This would result in a new content type, a discoverable resource template.  It defines the parameter that a key may contain - resource kind, yeild range, key-count range, eligible realm tags, flavor.  And then on a successful discovery the template gets generated.

Number of keys generated per discovery is rolled from the template parameters

### 10.3 Keys
#### 10.3.1 Summary
Keys grant its holder access to actions on a discovered resource that may not otherwise exist in the realm: gathering a rare resource type, combat sessions in a newly discovered monster camp, etc.  This access is private to the holder of the key.

#### 10.3.2 Key is the Node
The key is the node that the actions are used against.  Each key is self-contained, it contains the resource definition and remaining yield.

- multiple keys from one discovery are all unique and independent - they share flavor, but carry their own yield. A discovery of 3 keys of 500 resources has granted 1500 resource units into the world.
- The values for resource type and remaining yield are readable from the key item itself
- Each action cycle depletes the counter on the key item, once depleted, the key no longer functions as a node.
  - Combat keys will deplete differently, combat keys will deplete as the monster target is spawned.
- Depleted keys remain in the holder's inventory until the player chooses to destroy the item, but may be kept as  a trophy

#### 10.3.3 Invalidated keys
Keys are scoped to the realm in which they were discovered.  When a realm's type transitions (evolving, morphing, or regressing), keys for that realm are depileted and become invalid.

Any keys currently in use when a realm transitions will still work until they players actions deplete them, or they stop they action that is using the key.   

#### 10.4.1 Instanced items
Keys are non-fungible and will have to work differently than commodity goods and will necessitate their own table. Even keys from the same discovery may generate differently rolled values for certain fields.  As such, keys must not stack and also need seperate market mechanics.  And each instanced item will take up a distinct inventory slot per item.

#### 10.5.1 Trading keys
Instanced items are not commodities and as such will need their own market mechanics. So a new market interface for instanced items will require:

- fixed-price, buyout only
- seller will still pay a tax
- A way to browse keys by realm and either goods category or exact goods name
- Only unbound, unused keys may be listed

## 11 Realm Travel and Visitation
### 11.1 Summary
Acting where you don't live.  The world is a map of realms, and a character may travel to a realm they do not call home, in order to sell to a starving realm, hunt its dangerous fauna, or develop a frontier realm.

Travelling itself is a skill, journeys cost time and supplies; active travellers get faster and more efficient.  

### 11.2 Moving
A characters movement mode is determined by the form of their estate.

- A player with a fixed estate may choose to visit another realm.
- A player with a mobile estate relocates that estate and the estate moves with the player, and as such, the players home realm moves along too.
  - A player with a mobile estate will generally not want to visit, and should want to relocate.
  - A player with a boat should not be able to relocate over land based realm edges

### 11.3 Travel is a Skill
#### 11.3.1 Journey as an action
A journey is a set of actions under the existing cycle framework.
- The player picks a destination realm
- The route is the cheapest path over the map graph
- Each edge of the route takes an action cycle

The player is always in a realm, there is no limbo, between-realms, state.

Offline travel resolves with the existing lazy cycle resolution.  The welcome back summary should mention where how far along a journey a player has gone and any supplies consumed.


#### 11.3.2 Travel leveling
Travel earns xp per edge travelled scaled by the edge's base time cost.  A longer road gives more xp than a shorter one.  Higher travel level tiers reduce journey time.

#### 11.4 Travel costs
Map edges own the cost of travel and any skill level gates and supply costs.  These travel costs also apply to the NPC haulers that handle treasury-to-treasury inter-realm trade.

The player traveller owns the movement profile:
Travellers that are visiting use a base travel profile - light rations and fairly quick
Mobile estate relocation travels based on the mobile estate type

### 11.5 Being Away From Home
#### 11.5.1 What visitors can do
There is no visitor malus.  Visitors may do any skill that is available in the realm.  Visitors may contribute to any projects underway in the realm.  And of course, trade at the realm market.

#### 11.5.2 Bonuses and caps
Estates bonuses stay with the estate.  As such mobile estates that relocate with the player provide its bonuses to the player.  However, a player that visits a realm and doesn't bring their mobile estate with them will not receive any estate bonuses.

Caps apply to the player and as such permanently travel with the character.

#### 11.5.3
There is one player inventory that the player carries everywhere, plus the market escrow slots.  There is no estate storage or goods that can be located in a pace other than the player inventory.

## 12 The Player
### 12.1 The new player
The new player should be able to join and get an easy introduction into the game's mechanics and as such:

A new player should be able to frictionlessly join the game as an anonymous person with a temporary account.  As such the new player will "awaken" on Tutorial Island.

### 12.2 Tutorial Island
2 ephemeral realms that are cordonned off from the rest of the game world that will teach the player to use the different skills to bootstrap themselves.
- Realm 1 - The player will be able to gather items to create a set of clothes and a club. Then the player can perform some combat to create some food.  Then the player can travel to Realm 2
- Realm 2 - Intro to realm upgrades - the player will need to quickly create a realm upgrade to craft a raft estate to sail to the real world.  Upon building a raft-making site and then building a raft, the player can then travel to at least 1 realm on the actual game world.
- Prior to actually leaving tutorial island, the player will be required to create an actual account
- The player should be hinted at how to do each step and should be able to complete each step in just a few action cycles

### 12.3 Player equipment
#### 12.3.1 Slots
Standard Equipment: head, chest, gloves, legs, feet
Jewelry: neck, left arm, right arm, left ring, right ring
Belt: carries consumable slots; may grant skill benefits
Cape: specialized xp-bonus slot
Weapons: left hand, right hand; 2 handed items will go into the right hand slot and make the left hand slot disabled
Combat Utility Slot: per-cycle consumabke -- ammunition (ranged), weapon oils (melee), magic components (magic)

#### 12.3.2 Weapons
- Melee - one-handed weapon + shield, or two one-handed weapons, or a two-handed weapon. Can have damage flavors: piercing, bludgeoning, slashing.  Opitonal utlity slot items that provide a small benefit
- Ranged - two-handed, requires ammunition in the utility slot
- Magic - one-handed wand + focus or two-handed staff.  optional utilty slot items have a moderate effect

Weapons modify their associated attack skill. two-handed weapons grant larger bonuses at the cost of the off-hand slot and slower attack speed.

#### 12.3.3 Non-weapon equipment slots
1. Standard equipment - grants Defense and Vigor.  Lower-tier armor is general purpose; higher-tier armor specializes toward one attack style and tier-locks on that skill
2. Jewelry may modify any skill
3. Belt - holds consumable item slots and may carry minor skill benefits
4. Cape - dedicated slot for xp-bonus items
5. Utility slot - per-combat cycle consumable for the appropriate attack style.

#### 12.3.4 Main-hand tools equipment for skilling
The weapon slot family extends to skilling tools. A main-hand axe or hammer or pickaxe to improve skill-cycle speed.

#### 12.3.5 Gear durability
Gear has no durability and as such lasts forever. Acquiring gear for the player is a milestone, not an industry.
For the producers, there are other items that will be used as fungible trade goods required by realms.
